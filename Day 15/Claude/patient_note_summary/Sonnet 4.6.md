# Patient Note Summary & Risk Flag Report

**Document Type:** Clinical Note Review  
**Date Generated:** 2026-06-18  
**Purpose:** Summarise patient note and identify clinical and documentation risks

## Patient Note Summary

A patient presented with a **2-day history of chest pain**. Vital signs were recorded as stable. A 12-lead ECG (EKG) was performed and reported as unremarkable. The patient was **discharged home** with ibuprofen (an NSAID) and advised to return or follow up if symptoms worsened. No troponin levels were ordered. The attending physician noted that time pressure from high emergency department (ED) volume influenced the encounter.

---

## Clinical Risks

### 🔴 HIGH — Troponin Not Ordered

**Issue:** Chest pain of 2 days' duration without troponin measurement represents a significant departure from standard ACS (Acute Coronary Syndrome) workup protocols.

**Why it matters:**
- Serial troponin testing is a cornerstone of ruling out myocardial infarction (MI), including NSTEMI.
- An unremarkable ECG does **not** exclude MI — up to 50% of NSTEMIs present with a normal or non-diagnostic ECG.
- The patient could have been discharged with an active or evolving cardiac event undetected.

**Risk level:** High — potential missed MI / patient safety event

---

### 🔴 HIGH — NSAID Prescribed for Undifferentiated Chest Pain

**Issue:** Ibuprofen was prescribed without a confirmed diagnosis.

**Why it matters:**
- NSAIDs are **contraindicated** in suspected or confirmed cardiac ischaemia as they can increase cardiovascular risk.
- If the chest pain has a musculoskeletal cause, NSAIDs may be appropriate — but this was not documented as the working diagnosis.
- Prescribing NSAIDs without ruling out a cardiac cause poses both a clinical and medico-legal risk.

**Risk level:** High — potential harm if cardiac aetiology present

---

### 🟡 MODERATE — Adequacy of Follow-Up Instructions

**Issue:** The patient was advised to "follow up if symptoms worsen," but no specific timeframe, destination (GP, ED, cardiology), or red-flag criteria were documented.

**Why it matters:**
- Vague return precautions may not prompt timely re-presentation in a deteriorating patient.
- Best practice requires explicit instructions: e.g., "Return to ED immediately if pain increases, spreads to the arm/jaw, or you develop shortness of breath or sweating."

**Risk level:** Moderate — inadequate safety netting

---

### 🟡 MODERATE — Time Pressure Noted but No Mitigation Documented

**Issue:** The attending acknowledged time pressure from ED volume as a factor in the encounter.

**Why it matters:**
- While noting time pressure is honest, it does not mitigate the standard of care obligations.
- There is no documentation of a risk-benefit discussion with the patient or a plan to compensate for the abbreviated workup (e.g., urgent outpatient cardiology referral, stress test).

**Risk level:** Moderate — does not constitute a clinical defence

---

## Documentation Risks

### 🔴 HIGH — No Working Diagnosis or Differential Documented

**Issue:** The note does not record a working diagnosis or a differential diagnosis list.

**Why it matters:**
- Without a documented differential (e.g., ACS, musculoskeletal, GORD, PE), it is not possible to demonstrate that the clinician considered and excluded serious conditions.
- This is a significant medico-legal vulnerability.

---

### 🟡 MODERATE — Rationale for Discharge Not Documented

**Issue:** There is no documented clinical reasoning explaining why the patient was deemed safe for discharge.

**Why it matters:**
- Documentation should reflect the clinician's thought process, especially when a potentially high-risk complaint (chest pain) is discharged without full workup.
- Absence of reasoning implies absence of consideration.

---

### 🟡 MODERATE — Abbreviated/Informal Language

**Issue:** The note uses shorthand (e.g., "Pt c/o", "×2 days", "EKG done") without structured formatting.

**Why it matters:**
- While shorthand is common, the note lacks a SOAP or structured format, which may impede continuity of care and audit trails.
- Abbreviations may be misinterpreted by other clinicians or in medico-legal review.

---

### 🟢 LOW — No Allergies or Medication History Noted

**Issue:** The note does not mention whether allergies or current medications were reviewed before prescribing ibuprofen.

**Why it matters:**
- Standard prescribing documentation requires allergy status and current medication review (especially important for NSAIDs, which interact with anticoagulants, antihypertensives, and other NSAIDs).

## Recommendations

1. **Immediately:** If this is a current/recent case, consider contacting the patient for urgent troponin testing and cardiology review.
2. **Clinician level:** Ensure standard ACS rule-out protocol (troponin × 2, ECG, risk stratification tool such as HEART or TIMI score) is followed for all chest pain presentations.
3. **Documentation level:** Adopt structured note templates (SOAP/SBAR) with mandatory fields for differential diagnosis, clinical reasoning, and specific discharge instructions.
4. **Governance level:** Flag for clinical incident review or mortality/morbidity meeting, particularly given the documented influence of systemic time pressure on clinical decision-making.
