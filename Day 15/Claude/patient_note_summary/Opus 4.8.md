# Patient Note Review: Chest Pain Presentation

> **Purpose:** Clinical and documentation risk review for clinician/QA use. This is decision-support commentary, not a substitute for the treating clinician's judgment or institutional protocol.

## Summary

A patient presented with a chief complaint of chest pain lasting 2 days. Vital signs were recorded as stable and an EKG was performed and read as unremarkable. The patient was discharged home with ibuprofen and instructed to return if symptoms worsened. No troponin was ordered. The attending documented that ED volume created time pressure.

## Clinical Risks

**Incomplete chest pain workup / ACS not excluded.** A single unremarkable EKG does not rule out acute coronary syndrome. NSTEMI and unstable angina frequently present with a normal or non-diagnostic EKG. Without cardiac biomarkers, the disposition rests on an incomplete evaluation.

**No troponin ordered.** For an adult with chest pain, serial troponins (and often serial EKGs) plus a structured risk-stratification tool (e.g., HEART score) are typically expected before a safe discharge. Their absence is the single most significant clinical gap here.

**Ibuprofen as the discharge medication.** NSAIDs are relatively contraindicated when ACS is on the differential — they carry cardiovascular risk and can mask ischemic symptoms, potentially delaying re-presentation. If a musculoskeletal or pleuritic etiology was the working diagnosis, that reasoning is not documented.

**No documented risk stratification or differential.** There is no record of cardiac risk factors, pain characterization (onset, quality, radiation, exertional component, associated symptoms), or the differential considered (cardiac vs. PE vs. GI vs. musculoskeletal). This makes the discharge decision hard to defend on clinical grounds.

**Vague return precautions.** "Follow up if symptoms worsen" lacks specific red-flag instructions, a defined follow-up plan/timeframe, and a named provider or setting.

## Documentation Risks

**The time-pressure note is a liability.** Recording that the workup occurred under time pressure due to ED volume creates a contemporaneous record suggesting the evaluation may have been abbreviated for non-clinical reasons. In the event of an adverse outcome, this is precisely the kind of statement that supports a deviation-from-standard-of-care argument. If the workup was genuinely adequate, the clinical justification — not the staffing pressure — is what belongs in the chart.

**Missing core chart elements.** The note omits a documented HPI, past medical history, medication reconciliation, physical exam, and vital sign values (only "stable" is recorded — actual numbers should appear). 

**No clinical reasoning for disposition.** There is no stated working diagnosis, no rationale for forgoing troponin, and no documentation of shared decision-making or that the patient understood and agreed to the discharge plan.

**EKG attribution unclear.** No record of who interpreted the EKG, when it was performed relative to symptom onset, or whether it was compared to a prior.

## Suggested Actions (for review)

1. Consider whether the patient meets criteria for recall or callback given the absence of biomarker testing.
2. If clinically appropriate, reconsider the ibuprofen recommendation pending exclusion of a cardiac cause.
3. Amend the chart to document the actual vitals, HPI, exam, differential, and the reasoning behind the disposition — and reconsider whether the staffing/time-pressure remark belongs in the medical record versus an internal operations channel.
4. Strengthen discharge instructions with specific return precautions and a concrete follow-up plan.
