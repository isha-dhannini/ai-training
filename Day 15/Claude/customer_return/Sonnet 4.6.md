## Recommendation

**Accept the return.**

---

## Reasoning

Yes, accept the return — and do so without friction. The policy window exists to protect the business from abuse, not to penalise loyal customers for circumstances outside their control, and this case fails every reasonable test for abuse: the defect was reported to have occurred on day 44, meaning the fault arose *within* the return window; the 2-day overage is explained by the customer being in transit, a credible and common real-world constraint; the order is verifiable in your system, removing any ambiguity about the transaction; and the customer has spent six years and 23 orders demonstrating consistent, genuine engagement with your brand. Refusing this return saves nothing material — the cost of processing one jacket return is trivially small — while the cost of the refusal is potentially significant: a loyal customer who feels penalised for a product defect and a travel schedule is a customer likely to defect, leave a negative review, and tell others. Rigid policy enforcement is appropriate when the facts suggest opportunism; here, the facts suggest the opposite. The right call is to honour the return, waive the 2-day overage as a goodwill exception, and note the case in the customer record so the exception is documented and not exploited again. Policies serve customers — when a policy outcome contradicts that purpose, the policy yields.

---

## Key Decision Factors

### Factors Supporting Acceptance

- **Defect occurred within policy window** — the zipper broke on day 44, inside the 45-day limit. The issue is not whether the customer is late; the issue is whether the *product failed in time*. It did.
- **2-day overage is de minimis** — a 4% overage caused by travel is not a material deviation. Most businesses apply a reasonable buffer informally; 2 days is well within any good-faith interpretation.
- **Order verified in system** — the absence of a physical receipt is irrelevant. The transaction is confirmed, removing fraud risk entirely.
- **6-year tenure, 23 orders** — this customer's lifetime value and loyalty history are well established. The relationship warrants the benefit of the doubt.
- **Product defect, not change of mind** — this is not a buyer's remorse return. A broken zipper on a jacket fewer than 50 days old is a quality issue, which in many jurisdictions carries statutory warranty protections independent of store policy.

### Factors That Could Support Caution

- **Policy is policy** — consistent enforcement prevents policy erosion and sets clear expectations for all customers.
- **No receipt** — mitigated entirely by system verification.
- **Unverified defect claim** — the zipper break cannot be independently confirmed prior to return. Mitigated by the customer's history and the plausibility of the claim.

### Why Caution Factors Are Outweighed

Strict policy enforcement has its place — primarily when there are signals of abuse, no verifiable history, or a pattern of exceptions with the same customer. None of those conditions apply here. The caution factors are either already mitigated (no receipt) or represent a level of scepticism disproportionate to the facts (doubting a 6-year customer's account of a zipper failure).

## Suggested Customer Communication

> "Thank you for reaching out, and we're sorry to hear about the zipper issue — that's not the experience we want you to have. We can see your order in our system and we're happy to process the return for you. We'll make an exception to the 45-day window given that the fault occurred during your trip. Please send the jacket back using the prepaid label we'll email you, and we'll arrange a replacement or full refund — whichever you prefer."

## Process Note

Document this exception in the customer record with the reason code: *product defect reported within window / travel delay*. This ensures the exception is traceable, prevents it from being used as a precedent by other agents without context, and protects the business if the same customer attempts a similar claim again without equivalent justification.
