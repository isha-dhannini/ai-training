# Customer Return Decision: Jacket Return (Day 47 of 45-Day Policy)

## Case Summary
- **Customer Tenure:** 6 years, 23 prior orders
- **Current Return Request:** 47 days post-purchase
- **Policy Window:** 45 days
- **Days Over Policy:** 2 days
- **Reason for Return:** Zipper defect on day 44
- **Mitigating Factor:** Customer traveling at time of defect discovery
- **Documentation:** No receipt, but order verified in system

---

## Decision: **ACCEPT THE RETURN** ✅

### Reasoning (One Paragraph):

Accept this return despite being 2 days outside the 45-day window. The customer reported a product defect (broken zipper) on day 44—within the policy period—but was travelling, which explains the 2-day delay in initiating the return. This is a **manufacturing defect, not buyer's remorse**, which is the core reason return windows exist. The 6-year customer relationship with 23 orders represents significant lifetime value (~$2,000-5,000+ depending on jacket price), and rejecting a legitimate defect claim over 2 days will likely damage retention more than the cost of one return. The customer has demonstrated loyalty and the 2-day overage is directly attributable to travel circumstances beyond their control, not negligence or indecision. Accepting positions your company as customer-centric and reasonable; declining creates PR risk ("company denies return for 2 days over defective zipper") with a high-value customer.

---

## Supporting Analysis

### Why This Isn't a Close Call:

| Factor | Impact | Weight |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Policy violation (2 days) | Technically outside window | Low |
| **Product defect vs. buyer's remorse** | **Defect = quality issue, not discretionary** | **High** |
| **Customer tenure (6 years, 23 orders)** | **High lifetime value; repeat purchaser** | **High** |
| **Mitigating circumstance (travel)** | **Legitimate reason for delay, not negligence** | **High** |
| **No receipt (but order verified)** | **System verification sufficient; not a fraud risk** | **Medium** |

### Risk Assessment:

**Risk of Accepting:** Loss of ~$50-150 (jacket cost) + $10-20 (return shipping). Potential: None; return is legitimate.

**Risk of Declining:** 
- Alienating 6-year customer → loss of future orders (~$300-500 annually)
- Negative word-of-mouth or public review ("Company won't honor defective zipper")
- Reputational damage on social media (likely given customer frustration)
- Potential net loss: $1,000+ in lifetime value

**Clear winner:** Accept the return.

---

## Operational Recommendations

1. **Approve the return immediately** — don't make customer ask twice
2. **Provide return label** — assume shipping cost given circumstances
3. **Document the defect** — inspect zipper failure, assess if design/batch issue
4. **Follow up with product quality** — if defect is widespread, escalate
5. **Send goodwill gesture** — 15-20% discount code for next purchase (rebuilds relationship)
6. **Update internal notes** — flag customer as "loyalty priority"; future edge cases should favor approval

---

## Policy Clarification Going Forward

This case reveals a policy gap: **Return windows should distinguish between buyer's remorse (strict enforcement) and product defects (flexible enforcement).** Consider updating policy language:

> "We accept returns within 45 days of purchase. Product defects reported within the return window may be returned beyond 45 days if reported with documentation within the policy period."

This protects both customer interests (defects) and company interests (prevents abuse of "buyer's remorse" loophole while protecting quality).

---

## Bottom Line

**Approve. The 2-day overage is immaterial; the defect and customer loyalty are material.** This is a decision that costs little and gains significant goodwill.
